General outline for project

Lock Out Tag Out Simulation:
Gives task bar including saftey steps. If a saftey step is done wrong or missed, a 
graphic pops up to tell the person that they failed and demonstrate that they put themselves
or others in danger. This pop-up describes what they did wrong and what the right task is. 
That step is then restarted and has to be performed correctly before moving onto the next step.

Lock Out Tag Out Saftey Steps:

1. Prepare and Identify Energy Sources
-Identify every energy source (electrical, hydraulic, pneumatic, mechanical, thermal, chemical).
-Review the machine‑specific energy control procedure.
-Determine the type and magnitude of energy and how to control it.
https://wcsafety.com/blogs/reference/how-to-perform-lockout-tagout 

2. Notify Affected Employees
-Inform operators and nearby personnel that the equipment will be shut down and locked out.
-Notification prevents accidental restarts.

3. Shut Down the Equipment
-Use the machine’s normal stopping procedure (not the disconnect switch).
-Ensure an orderly shutdown to avoid creating hazards.

4. Isolate All Energy Sources
-Open disconnects, close valves, block mechanical parts, blank lines, unplug equipment as required.
-Every identified energy source must have a corresponding isolation action.

5. Apply Locks and Tags
-Each authorized worker applies their own lock and tag.
-Tags must identify who applied them and why.
-Group lockout uses a lockbox so each worker controls their own protection.

6. Release or Restrain Stored Energy
-Bleed hydraulic/pneumatic lines, discharge capacitors, block elevated parts, release spring tension, allow hot surfaces to cool.
-Isolation stops new energy; this step removes what’s already stored.

7. Verify Zero‑Energy State
-Attempt to start the machine using normal controls (then return them to “off”).
-Use test instruments where appropriate (e.g., voltage tester).
-This is the most commonly skipped and most critical step.

8. Perform the Work
-Only after verification is complete may servicing or maintenance begin.


Fork Lift Saftey Simulation:
1. Visual Inspection

☐ Tires in good condition, properly inflated (if pneumatic)
 ☐ No excessive tire wear or damage
 ☐ Forks free from cracks, bends, or excessive wear
 ☐ Mast and chains in good condition
 ☐ Load backrest secure
 ☐ Overhead guard undamaged
 ☐ No visible hydraulic leaks
 ☐ No fuel, oil, or coolant leaks
 ☐ Battery secure and charged (electric forklifts)
 ☐ LPG/propane tank secured, no leaks (if applicable)
 ☐ Capacity plate present and readable
 ☐ Safety decals and warning labels legible


2. Operational Inspection

☐ Seat belt functions properly
 ☐ Horn operates correctly
 ☐ Backup alarm operational (if equipped)
 ☐ Lights working properly
 ☐ Brakes function effectively
 ☐ Parking brake holds forklift in place
 ☐ Steering operates smoothly
 ☐ Hydraulic controls function correctly
 ☐ Lift, lower, and tilt functions operate properly
 ☐ Gauges and indicators working
 ☐ No unusual noises or vibrations


3. Safety Checks

☐ Fire extinguisher available (if required)
 ☐ Operator compartment clean and clear
 ☐ Mirrors adjusted and undamaged
 ☐ Load capacity understood and visible
 ☐ Emergency procedures reviewed
 ☐ No unauthorized modifications to forklift


4. Operating Area Inspection

☐ Travel paths clear of obstacles
 ☐ Adequate lighting in work area
 ☐ Floor surfaces free from major hazards
 ☐ Pedestrian walkways marked and visible
 ☐ Loading docks and ramps safe for operation
 ☐ Aisles and intersections clearly marked
